## Post 2: Educational Value Post (Share knowledge first, mention app naturally)

**Title:** Why falling barometric pressure triggers feeding in bass (and how to use it)

Ever notice the bite turns on right before a storm? There's real science behind it.

When barometric pressure falls rapidly, it signals an approaching weather front. Fish sense this through their swim bladder — the pressure change makes them feel uncomfortable at their current depth, which triggers a feeding response before the front hits. It's biological, not magical.

Here's what to look for:
- **Pressure dropping 0.5+ inHg in 6 hours** = strong feeding trigger
- **Pressure dropping steadily over 12-24 hours** = extended bite window
- **Pressure rising sharply after a front** = bite shuts down (fish become lethargic)

The pre-frontal window is often the BEST fishing you'll have all week. If you see pressure falling, go NOW.

I actually built this into my app's scoring engine (FishSmart Pro) — it tracks pressure trends in real-time and factors them into the bite score so you know exactly when the pre-frontal bite is happening.

But you don't need the app to use this knowledge. Just check your weather app for pressure trends and hit the water when it's falling. 🎣
